#+title: Homescan vs RMS prices: what differs, and why
#+date: 2026-10-19

prices: what are the differences?  Do they depend on coupons, reporting,
...?  Are there important differences in the two price sources?"  This
note answers from the Kilts manuals (sections 1-5, documented facts with

* 1. The two price concepts (documented)

What each file's =prices= is (D4 section 2): revenue / ounces per (t, m, j).

- Homescan (the panel): revenue = sum of =total_price_paid= over the
  sample households' purchase records (AM:636-645), unprojected.
  =total_price_paid= is "Total price paid for all units, before discounts";
  =coupon_value= is "Total discount for amount due to coupon"; "To calculate
  the final price paid by panelist, subtract =coupon_value= from
  =total_price_paid=" (CP p. 27, Purchases file, and Note 1; the same for
  2021+ on pp. 40-41; FAQ 24, p. 62).  The panel uses =total_price_paid=
  as is, so its price is *gross of coupons*.
- RMS: revenue = sum over store-weeks of units x price / prmult.  =price=
  is "The volume weighted average price of the product for that week"
  (RMS p. 15); "NielsenIQ uses the volume weighted price of all sales for
  that good in that week" (RMS p. 16, note G).  So it is a weekly average
  over transactions, not the modal or the shelf price.  Multi-unit deals:
  "3 for $1" is prmult = 3, price = $1.00; "The unit price would be
  price/prmult" (RMS p. 6 and p. 16, note E).  Sales tax is typically not
  included (note H).  Zero prices are not allowed: free goods are coded
  1 cent (p. 17, note N).
- What the RMS weekly price includes (RMS p. 16, note F): "Retailer
  discounts and specials (e.g. discounts associated with the use of a
  retailer's loyalty card or coupons) are factored into the weighted
  average weekly price.  Manufacturer coupons are not factored into the
  weighted average weekly price."
- RMS values can be edited by NielsenIQ: in under ~1% of cases prices are
  changed (about a quarter of those are outliers re-imputed from history,
  three quarters are unit-basis alignments); under ~3% of store-weeks are
  wholly imputed; no flag marks them (RMS p. 16, note D).

So at the level of one transaction both sources are, by construction,
*after* in-store and loyalty-card reductions and *before* manufacturer
coupons, *where the Homescan price comes from store data* (section 5).
Where the panelist types the price, the instruction is to "enter the price
paid (prior to any coupons or deals)" (CP p. 20; FAQ 21, p. 62); whether
"deals" there means shelf price before a temporary reduction, or only
before a coupon-like deduction at the register, the manual does not say.

* 2. Coupons (documented)

- Homescan records the panelist's coupon as =coupon_value=, a dollar
  discount on the record (CP p. 9, item 2d; p. 21; p. 27).  The manual does
  not say whether it covers manufacturer coupons only or also retailer
  coupons; it is what the panelist enters.
- RMS has no coupon field.  Retailer coupons are inside the weekly price;
  manufacturer coupons are not (RMS p. 16, note F).
- So both panel prices, as built, are gross of manufacturer coupons:
  the panel's =prices= does not subtract =coupon_value=.  Netting coupons
  out of Homescan would move it away from RMS's concept, not toward it,
  unless =coupon_value= is mostly retailer coupons (which RMS nets).
- ELN (p. 15, fn. 14): their price regressions (who pays less for the
  same item) come out "qualitatively similar" with prices net of coupons.

* 3. Deals (documented)

- Homescan: =deal_flag_uc= = 1 if "the item was perceived by the consumer
  to be on a 'deal'" (CP p. 9, item 2c; p. 21; layout p. 27).  It is the
  panelist's perception, not a price fact.  Loyalty-card discounts: "If
  the panelist has a frequent shopper card and is aware that a particular
  item was discounted for card members, they can indicate this as a deal
  but are not required to do so" (CP p. 21).  A zero =total_price_paid=
  with a deal flag is a valid "free goods" price; without one Nielsen
  advises treating it as missing (FAQ 22, p. 62).
- RMS: no deal flag.  Feature and display flags exist for an "audited"
  subset of stores only (RMS pp. 6, 16-17, notes J-L); there is no TPR
  indicator (note M).  Multi-unit deals are coded through prmult (note E).

* 4. Coverage and the store link (documented)

- Homescan covers purchases "from any outlet" (CP p. 9).  RMS covers only
  the stores of retailers that agreed to share with Kilts: "not every
  NielsenIQ retail cooperator has agreed to share their scanner data with
  the Kilts Center" (RMS p. 7).  So there are three kinds of store, not
  two: (i) in Kilts RMS; (ii) NielsenIQ POS cooperators that do not share
  with Kilts; (iii) stores NielsenIQ has no POS data for.
- Trips carry both =retailer_code= (the chain, banner level) and
  =store_code_uc= ("ID code for each individual store.  This
  store_code_uc can be used to link consumer panel data with the retail
  scanner data.  Note that a value of zero represents 'missing'", CP
  pp. 25-26; also p. 9).  "Store code and store zip are provided only for a
  subset of trips" (CP p. 9).
- RMS =stores= carry =retailer_code= "inferred from the Consumer Panel
  Dataset" (RMS p. 8); it "is the same as the retailer_code field in the
  Consumer Panel dataset" and is blank for a store with no panel
  transactions (RMS pp. 10-11, notes A, C).  In the classic years 42% of
  store-years have no retailer_code for that reason (RMS p. 23).
- AUDIT-2022 (2022 only): 83.6% of Homescan cereal dollars are on trips
  with a store code; 38.4% are at an RMS-2022 store by store code; 42.8%
  at an RMS retailer_code; the single largest uncovered chain is 21.1%;
  138 of RMS's 144 chains appear in the trips.  Trips' store codes exist
  essentially only for NielsenIQ-scanned stores, so "has a store code" is
  close to "NielsenIQ has POS data for this store".

* 5. Homescan prices imputed from store data (documented)

This is the point that decides whether the sources share a price concept.

- CP p. 9 (data entry sequence): "Price - entered only if the product was
  purchased at a store that doesn't provide NielsenIQ with retail scanner
  data.  Otherwise, the price is filled in by NielsenIQ."
- CP p. 20 (Price Determination): "if the outlet shopped is a retailer for
  which NielsenIQ receives electronic point of sale (POS) data, the price
  prompt is bypassed.  In this case, the price reported is an average
  weighted price for the item that week in that particular store."  Also:
  "For certain harder to recruit-maintain demographics, panelists are not
  required to enter prices for any retailers.  These prices are then
  imputed using POS data as well as other sources of data (prices ranges,
  median prices from other panelists etc)."  And: "NielsenIQ does not
  distinguish prices entered by the panelist from those that are imputed".
  Repeated in FAQ 21 (pp. 61-62).
- RMS p. 15 (note B) and p. 16 (note C): "In most cases for panel data,
  panelists do not enter prices for retailers for which NielsenIQ receives
  scanner data.  Prices are ascribed from the scanner data for the
  specific store a panelist shopped in the reported week.  As such, prices
  should generally be consistent for scanning data and panel data";
  exceptions are retailers where NielsenIQ does not ascribe (panelists
  enter prices) and late transmissions, where "the imputed price is based
  on the following week's scanning data".
- ELN (pp. 4, 11-12), validating 2004 Homescan against one retailer's
  transaction records: "when available, Nielsen uses store-level (average)
  prices instead of the actual price paid by the household".  The two
  prices disagree in about half the cases; their correlation is 0.88; the
  error is 21.8% of the variance of the Homescan price (8.5% where no
  loyalty-card discount was offered, correlation 0.96).  Where a card
  discount applied, Homescan over-reports the card-holder's price (mean
  error over 14%), because the imputed weekly average mixes card and
  non-card buyers (p. 11).  Card use: 75-80% of transactions; about 60% of
  transacted items carried a card discount at that retailer (p. 11).

Consequence (documented): at a store whose POS data NielsenIQ receives,
the Homescan price for a UPC-week is the same object as the RMS price for
that store-UPC-week, the store's weekly volume-weighted average, not what
the household paid.  This holds for every NielsenIQ POS store, including
those of chains that do not share with Kilts, so "outside RMS" is not
"panelist-entered".  Section 6.5 checks it record by record.

#+title: Why homog's theta differs between RMS and the panel (theta-gap check)
#+date: 2026-10-19

Requested by EL after D4/D6 (2026-10-19).  D6 found the default =homog=
theta at 2.2568 (SE 0.0576) on RMS (=homog.rms=) against 1.2222 (SE 0.0669)
on the panel over the same window (=homog.win2006-2024.baseQ1Y2006=).  This
note re-estimates the same specification on subsets that each isolate one
candidate cause.  Numbers first; the interpretation in the last two sections
is a hypothesis for EL, not a finding.

* Method

- Driver: CerealDemand =src/rms_theta_gap.py= (branch =rms=; 072cc5d,
  c9ea276).  Each experiment runs =build_one(Spec("homog", source="rms",
  ...))=, the production path with its defaults (two-step clustered GMM,
  s = (t, m) clusters, K = 2, LF instruments, two-way FE, 2,000-draw
  cluster-wild J), in its own subprocess with its own data directory and law
  store.  Panel experiments (P*) pass the panel's own cells through the same
  reader; the homog data path reads only revenue, prices, the five nutrients
  and brand_descr.
- Route check, P0: the panel's 2006-2024 cells through this route give theta
  1.2222 (SE 0.0669), theta_std 0.1563, sigma_logp 0.1279, identical to the
  D6 panel law.  The route changes nothing.
- theta is in original units: theta = theta_std / sigma_logp, where theta_std
  = exp(phi) and sigma_logp is the sample sd of log price.  Subsetting moves
  sigma_logp, so both are reported.
- Balanced stores (R5): stores with module-1344 movement in all 19 years,
  2006-2024 (=src/rms_store_sets.py=, d17f4ec): 17,557 stores, 53-69% of each
  year's dollars (69% in 2017, 55% in 2018).  Panel built with
  =src/rms_market_panel.py --stores ... --outdir ...= (opt-in; the default
  build is unchanged).
- DMA split (R6/P6, R7/P7): DMAs whose mean panel =n_hh= (Homescan sample
  households) is at or above the median, 511, are "large" (33 of 65).  Whole
  markets, so every brand in a market is kept.
- DMA-size tertiles (R8-R10, P8-P10): the same mean =n_hh=, cut into thirds
  (22, 21 and 22 DMAs; mean sample 256-373, 375-743 and 763-2,758
  households).
- Data and laws: =Data/NielsenScanner/rms/theta_gap/<exp>/= (group-only),
  summary in =theta_gap/summary.json=; never the shared =var/laws=.

* What the runs show

1. *Cell composition does not explain the gap.*  On identical cells, RMS gives
   2.33 and the panel 1.23 (R1, P1).  Dropping the 4,591 RMS-only cells
   raises RMS's theta slightly.
2. *The RMS store universe does not explain it.*  With the same 17,557 stores
   in every year, RMS theta is 2.43 (R5), not lower than 2.26.
3. *Homescan sampling noise does not explain it either.*  By DMA-size tertile
   (smallest Homescan samples first):

   | tertile | panel theta | RMS theta | RMS / panel | panel sigma_logp | RMS sigma_logp |
   |-|-|-|-|-|-|
   | 1 (small) | 0.92 | 2.23* | 2.4 | 0.145 | 0.103 |
   | 2 | 1.24 | 2.17 | 1.8 | 0.129 | 0.106 |
   | 3 (large) | 2.63* | 4.85 | 1.8 | 0.107 | 0.093 |

   (* did not converge.)  The panel's theta rises with Homescan sample size
   while its sd of log price falls, which is what noise would do.  But RMS's
   theta rises in the largest tertile too, and RMS stays about 1.8x the
   panel even where Homescan's samples are biggest.  In tertile 2, the only
   pair where both runs converged, the ratio is 1.75.  (The coverage
   test below qualifies this: thinning the panel's cells lowers its
   theta, so sampling noise does pull the panel's theta down; what the
   tertiles rule out is noise as the whole story.)
4. *theta varies with market size within each source.*  Both sources give a
   much higher theta in the largest third of DMAs (panel 2.63, RMS 4.85)
   than in the rest.  A single theta pooled over all markets averages over
   that variation.  The very large J statistics in every run (p at the
   bootstrap floor) fit a model that is misspecified across markets.

Superseded or weak runs:
- *The two-way DMA split (R6-P7) is superseded by the tertiles.*  Its
  near-agreement in large DMAs (RMS 2.53, panel 2.28) mixed tertiles 2 and 3.
  An earlier version of this note read it as "the sources agree where
  Homescan samples are large"; the tertiles do not support that.
- The thick-cell pair (R2, P2) selects cells on panel =n_transactions=, a
  quantity, so it selects on the outcome.  That biases both levels.
- The window split (R3/P3, R4/P4) has three of four runs unconverged.  RMS's
  full-period theta lies above both of its windows (1.52, 1.29), but with
  R3 and R4 unconverged that is not established.

* Open puzzle: subsample thetas do not average to the full sample

RMS's full-sample theta (2.26) is below both of its DMA halves (2.53,
2.80).  By tertile it sits next to the lower two (2.23, 2.17), while the top
third gives 4.85.  A two-way-FE GMM theta is not a weighted average of
subsample thetas: splitting the markets refits the product fixed effects
separately in each subsample, and the full-sample estimate leans on
whichever cells carry the most residual price variation.

* What this leaves (for EL)

What remains is a difference in what the two sources measure.  The panel
records the unit value Homescan households paid, across all retailers,
including the non-reporting chain (21% of cereal dollars).  RMS records
shelf prices at the reporting stores.  D4 found that about half of each
source's residual price variation, after product and (t, m) fixed effects,
is not shared with the other.  The panel's unit value (revenue / servings)
also shares its noise with the expenditure share, the division-bias channel
the CerealDemand handoff's "THE BLP-CES STORY" section discusses.

So the evidence does not say the panel's declared =homog= theta of 1.23 is
understated, as an earlier version of this note suggested.  It says that
theta depends on the source and on market size, and that a pooled theta
hides heterogeneity across markets.  Which price concept the paper's theta
should rest on, and whether theta should be allowed to vary by market
size, are EL's calls.

What it shows:
- The source gap is larger under het than under homog.  The median theta_i
  is 14.3 on RMS against 4.2 on the panel (3.4x in original units; 2.7x
  in standardized units, 1.44 vs 0.54).  homog's ratio was 1.85.
- RMS stays above the panel in every tertile where both converged (9.5 vs
  3.9 in tertile 2; 19.1 vs 6.8 in tertile 3).
- P8 (panel, smallest DMAs) did not converge: its theta_i are bunched near
  120, the theta -> infinity corner the het spec comments warn of

  panel's.  Whether RMS het sits near the same corner without the
  optimizer reporting it is not established; the J statistics reject
  everywhere (cluster-wild p = 0.002, the floor for these draws).
- Note that het's demographics (DEMOG_PI and the Bartik demographics) come
  from Homescan in both sources, so they cannot explain the source gap.

Why RMS gives a much larger theta is the price-concept question (EL,
2026-10-19), taken up in =docs/rms-prices.org=.

* Coverage test: homog on panel cells rebuilt from purchase subsets (2026-10-19)

The price note (=docs/rms-prices.org=) found that only 46.6% of the
panel's cereal dollars are spent at chains that report to RMS.  Its
proposed test: re-estimate =homog= on panel cells rebuilt from purchases at
those chains only.  If coverage explained the gap, theta on those cells
would move toward RMS's 2.26.  EL asked for the test (2026-10-19).

Method:
- Cells: =src/rms_prices.py hs --outdir Data/NielsenScanner/rms/prices=
  (e504245) rebuilds every panel cell from the raw purchases.  Its
  all-purchases variant reproduces =market_panel.parquet= exactly (214,637
  cells, maximum relative price difference 0, no =n_transactions=
  mismatch).  Each variant re-aggregates servings, revenue and
  =n_transactions= from a subset of purchases; price = revenue / servings.
- Covered: the trip's store is an RMS store with cereal movement in that
  calendar year, or its =retailer_code= is one of those stores' chains.
- Placebo: a seeded random split of trips, each trip kept with probability
  equal to the covered share of dollars (46.6%).  H4 is the trips it left
  out (53.4%, the uncovered share).
- Driver: CerealDemand =src/rms_theta_gap.py= H0-H4 (7e74c2f, eaef4f6).
  Each variant's servings, revenue, =n_transactions= and prices replace
  the panel's on the cells it reaches.  The other columns are the panel's,
  and the homog path reads only revenue, prices, the nutrients and
  brand_descr.  H0 (all purchases) writes a file identical to P0's in
  every column and reproduces its theta.

| H2 | random trips, H1's dollar share (placebo) | 207,120 | 0.7253 | 0.0456 | 0.1108 | 0.1527 | 1604.3 | *False* |
| H3 | purchases outside RMS chains (53.4%) | 206,181 | 0.9987 | 0.0594 | 0.1474 | 0.1476 | 2365.4 | True |
| H4 | the other random trips, H3's share | 209,088 | 0.9838 | 0.0519 | 0.1459 | 0.1483 | 2102.3 | True |

All runs have 4,940 (t, m) clusters and 16 J degrees of freedom; every
cluster-wild J p-value is at the bootstrap floor.

What it shows:
1. *Coverage does not explain the gap.*  Panel cells from purchases at RMS
   chains give theta 0.71, further below RMS (2.26) than the full panel
   (1.22).
2. *A random set of trips of the same size gives the same theta.*  At
   46.6% of dollars: covered 0.71, random 0.73.  At 53.4%: uncovered 1.00,
   random 0.98.  Which retailers the purchases came from does not move
   theta beyond what removing that many purchases does.  H2 did not
   converge, so the cleaner pair is H3/H4, both converged.
3. *Thinning the panel's cells lowers theta steeply.*  Building cells from
   about half the purchases raises the sd of log price (0.128 to
   0.148-0.171) and lowers theta from 1.22 to 0.71-1.00.  That is what
   noise in the unit values would do: it attenuates theta.  This qualifies
   point 3 of "What the runs show".  The panel's theta does depend on how
   many purchases stand behind each cell, so Homescan sampling noise does
   pull it down.  It is not established that the full panel is noisy
   enough for this to account for the RMS gap.  The tertiles show RMS
   about 1.8x the panel even in the largest DMAs, and RMS's theta rises
   with market size too.
4. The response to thinning is uneven: 0.73 at 46.6% and 0.98 at 53.4% of
   dollars (one random split, and H2 unconverged), then 1.22 at 100%.
   Read its size as rough.

Not run (EL's call): a thinning curve, homog on several random splits of
trips at, say, 25/50/75% with a few seeds each (about 4 minutes per run on
CPU).  It would show whether the panel's theta levels off as cells
thicken, and where.

* Thinning curve (2026-10-19)

=homog= on panel cells rebuilt from a random share of trips (=src/rms_prices.py
thin=, ab38383; CerealDemand =src/rms_theta_gap.py= T<pct>_<k>, f979a00).
Each seed draws one uniform per trip, so one seed's subsets are nested.
Keeping every trip reproduces the panel exactly.

| share of trips | theta, seeds 1 / 2 / 3 | mean | sigma_logp |
|-|-|-|-|
| 25% | 0.4495 / 0.4694 / 0.4964 | 0.472 | 0.177-0.178 |
| 50% | 0.8613 / 0.8828 / 0.8032 | 0.849 | 0.150-0.151 |
| 75% | 1.0095 / 1.0918 / 1.0349* | 1.045 | 0.136 |
| 100% (P0) | 1.2222 | 1.222 | 0.128 |
| RMS (D6) | 2.2568 | | 0.101 |

(* T75_3 did not converge; the other eight did.)  SEs are 0.04-0.06.

What it shows:
1. The panel's theta rises steadily with the number of purchases behind
   each cell, and the seed-to-seed spread is small (at most 0.08).
2. It has not levelled off at the full panel: the steps are +0.38, +0.20
   and +0.18 per 25 points of trips.  So cell sampling noise in the
   panel's unit values attenuates its theta, and the noise-free value is
   above 1.22.
3. Whether that value reaches RMS's 2.26 is not established; no
   extrapolation has been fitted.  The DMA-size tertiles (RMS about 1.8x
   the panel even in the largest DMAs) are evidence that it may not.
